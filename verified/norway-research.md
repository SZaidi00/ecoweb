# Region: Norway — mainland, fjords and fresh waters
**Researched:** 2026-11 · **Status:** complete (Stage 1 — awaiting verification)

**Repo context at sweep time:** 15 webs live. Biome saturation: marine 7 (saturated), freshwater 3 (saturated), grassland 3 (saturated), estuary 2, desert 1, wetland 1; zero alpine/tundra and zero boreal-forest webs. ### Midtdalsbreen pioneer glacier-foreland web, Hardangerjøkulen, Norway — `midtdalsbreen-pioneer-web`
- **Biome / Region:** Alpine glacier-foreland terrestrial invertebrate web with a pond-to-land subsidy · South-central Norway · **Lat/Lng (approx):** 60.575, 7.461
- **Source:** Hågvar, S. & Pedersen, A. (2015). Food choice of invertebrates during early glacier foreland succession. *Arctic, Antarctic, and Alpine Research* 47(3): 561–572. DOI [10.1657/AAAR0014-046](https://doi.org/10.1657/AAAR0014-046); [open BioOne PDF](https://bioone.org/journals/arctic-antarctic-and-alpine-research/volume-47/issue-3/AAAR0014-046/Food-Choice-of-Invertebrates-During-Early-Glacier-Foreland-Succession/10.1657/AAAR0014-046.pdf). Same-site carbon-subsidy evidence: Hågvar, S. & Ohlson, M. (2013). Ancient carbon from a melting glacier gives high 14C age in living pioneer invertebrates. *Scientific Reports* 3: 2820. DOI [10.1038/srep02820](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep02820); Hågvar, S., Ohlson, M. & Brittain, J.E. (2016). A melting glacier feeds aquatic and terrestrial invertebrates with ancient carbon and supports early succession. *AAAR* 48: 551–562. DOI [10.1657/AAAR0016-027](https://doi.org/10.1657/AAAR0016-027). Synthesis with web figure: Hågvar, S., Ohlson, M. & Flø, D. (2017). Animal Successional Pathways for about 200 Years Near a Melting Glacier. DOI [10.5772/intechopen.68192](https://doi.org/10.5772/intechopen.68192), Fig. 16 and Table 3.
- **Data access:** **ASSUMED (strong)** — the full 2015 paper was obtained and read. Table 1 links four consumers to bryophyte taxa; Table 3 is a food-source matrix for 11 consumers across biofilm, fungal hyphae, bryophytes, vascular plants, invertebrates and ancient-carbon-derived chironomids. The 2017 open chapter reproduces Table 3 and provides a whole-web Fig. 16. Values have not been independently transcribed or checked at Stage 2; no repository deposit was found.
- **Raw size:** 11 gut-resolved consumer species (five Collembola, five beetles, one harvestman), six established bryophyte taxa in the detailed survey, plus resource/subsidy categories; link count not yet extracted · **Aggregation plan:** → ~20–23 nodes (retain all 11 consumer species and six bryophyte taxa; add biofilm/diatoms, fungi, chironomid midges, ancient organic carbon, vascular plants and inblown detritus as source nodes only where Fig. 16/Table 3 supports them)
- **Data type:** qualitative presence/absence links with selected semi-quantitative gut-content percentages · **Provenance:** empirical same-site observational program; Stage 2 must decide whether adding the 2013/2016 ancient-carbon evidence makes the final web `composite`
- **Scores:** data 2 · story 3 · geography 3 · cost 3 · recognition 1 = **12**
- **Tier:** **1 (high-curation-risk)** · **Notes:** A distinctive “ecosystem assembled from invisible resources” story: diatom biofilm and tiny mosses start local food chains almost immediately, while pond midges carry ancient glacier carbon to terrestrial beetles and harvestmen. Removing the midge subsidy or pioneer mosses offers a clear cascade narrative and fills mainland Norway plus alpine terrestrial coverage. Do not claim that spiders’ diets were measured — spider guts were not examined. The 15-node minimum depends on retaining the source’s bryophyte resolution and distinct carbon pools rather than collapsing everything to “plants/detritus.” Stage 2 must transcribe only published links, resolve the empirical/composite badge, and ensure no unsupported edges are added from general natural history.

## Rejected in sweep

- **Norwegian and Barents Seas NorBar / Planque et al. (2024), DOI 10.1016/j.pocean.2024.103361** — valid published food-web assessment with supplementary input data, but overlaps the already verified `barents-sea` lineage and fails marine saturation; not duplicated in this ledger.
- **Barents spatial/metaweb versions (Kortsch et al.; Dryad `dryad.k04q2kd`; Jordán et al. 2024)** — same existing regional candidate, 238-node metaweb in the latest version, and marine saturation; no duplicate row.
- **Balsfjord pelagic web (Hopkins, Grotnes & Eliassen 1989)** — pelagic-only, older conference model, not a complete community web for the current product; marine saturation also applies.
- **Lake Mjøsa/Randsfjorden siloxane webs and Oslofjord/Åsefjorden contaminant webs** — contaminant-transfer sampling chains, not resolved whole-community edge matrices; freshwater/marine saturation also applies.
- **Dovre integrated gyrfalcon–ptarmigan–rodent model (Slettenhaar et al. 2026)** — valuable long-term demographic model but only three modeled species, far below richness and not a community matrix.
- **Scandinavian scavenger-gradient dataset (Gomo et al. 2020)** — baited-camera scavenger guild, not a resolved whole food web.
- **Hardangervidda general monitoring / Dovre food-web papers** — predator-specific and indirect-interaction studies were found, but no additional whole-community matrix beyond the existing Varanger/COAT lead.
- **Eastern Norwegian Sea slope-fish and Skagerrak deep-fish webs** — useful diet literature for model parameterization but fish-assemblage subwebs rather than complete community matrices; marine saturation also applies.
- **Kongsfjorden fecal-DNA tundra web and other Svalbard systems** — outside this extension’s scope and already represented by the tundra-boreal sweep’s Svalbard candidates.
- **German introduced-tree soil webs and multi-country riparian fatty-acid studies** — repository false positives/non-Norwegian study scope; no Norwegian whole-web matrix.
