# Region: Ireland — terrestrial, freshwater, estuarine & surrounding marine waters
**Researched:** 2026-11-22 · **Status:** complete (Stage 1 — awaiting independent verification)

**Repo context at sweep time:** 16 webs live. Biome saturation: marine 6 (saturated), freshwater 3 (saturated), grassland 3 (saturated), estuary 2, wetland 1, desert 1, temperate terrestrial forest 0. Scope: the island of Ireland, including the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland, its terrestrial and freshwater systems, estuaries and locally bounded coastal webs. Country-scale scope is consistent with the earlier Malaysia, Pakistan, Japan and country-level Europe candidates; it is not a continent-scale sweep. Out of scope as new candidates: the already-recorded Lough Hyne, Irish Sea and Celtic Sea models, non-trophic networks, and taxon-only interaction compilations with no bounded community web.

**Previous records:** `lough-hyne` is already Tier 2 and verified in `verified/europe.md`; `irish-sea-ecopath` is already appendix and verified there. The Celtic Sea shelf candidate also covers waters southwest of Ireland. Their verified columns and files are unchanged. This extension sweep found one new buildable terrestrial candidate and does not duplicate those ledger rows.

**Outcome:** one Tier 2 candidate, `gearagh-forest-floor`, passes all discovery-level hard gates. Its complete qualitative network is available through GATEWAy/Netzschleuder (122 taxa / 383 links), with a published primary source and a strong river-island fragmentation story. `lough-neagh-ecopath` has an open 20-group weighted diet matrix but is appendix-only under the freshwater-saturation gate and is grey literature. `dublin-bay-energy-flow` is a published weighted network but has only 8–12 compartments. Lough Feeagh and Carlingford Lough are rejected as current whole-web candidates. No Tier 1 candidate was found.

- **Tier:** 2 · **Notes:** Best new Irish candidate. The Gearagh's woodland islands sit within a braided river floodplain, giving a concrete fragmentation story: island area, seasonal species turnover and generalist predators alter web structure. Basal-resource removal and loss of mobile predators offer different cascade paths without inventing interaction strengths. Curation risks are substantial but tractable: most links are literature-derived rather than local gut observations; parasitic links need consistent treatment; and the current Netzschleuder mirror labels the web `terrestrial belowground`, while a Brose supplement search result calls it `terrestrial aboveground`.
