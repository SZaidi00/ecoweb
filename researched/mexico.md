# Region: Mexico — terrestrial, freshwater, estuarine & coastal waters
**Researched:** 2026-11-23 · **Status:** complete (Stage 1 — awaiting independent verification)

**Scope:** Mexico was treated as one country-scale target, consistent with earlier country sweeps and narrower than the approved “Mexico & Mesoamerica” North America gap-fill sub-region. This is an extension, not a re-run of existing Mexico records. Coral-reef systems remain owned by the coral-reef sweep (`banco-chinchorro` was rejected there for inaccessible appendices); Gulf of California desert islands remain in the verified desert sweep; Guaymas Basin vents/seeps remain in the deep-sea sweep. Those entries were not re-scored or changed. Non-trophic mutualisms and host–parasitoid-only networks are out of scope.

**Repo context at sweep time:** 17 live webs and no implemented Mexico pin. Biome saturation: marine 6, freshwater 4 and grassland 3 (all saturated), estuary 2, wetland 1, desert 1, tropical terrestrial 0. Bounded estuarine/mangrove models therefore have the clearest route through the gates. Only one additional estuary would bring that biome to three live webs, so the Tier 1 entries below are ranked alternatives, not a recommendation to build all of them.

**Outcome:** 12 retained candidates — eight Tier 1, no Tier 2, four appendix. Celestún Lagoon is the top candidate: the open 2003 report prints a 19-group weighted diet matrix, so it fits the node window without aggregation. Términos Lagoon has two distinct, well-documented alternatives: a 20-group whole-lagoon model and a 16-group seagrass/mangrove-habitat model. Chantuto–Panzacola adds the strongest flagship story (crocodilians and an overexploited gar) but requires aggregation from 34 groups. No resolved whole-forest or whole-lake Mexican web passed all gates. All accessibility labels are Stage 1 assessments, not independent verification.

**Access terminology:** `ASSUMED` means a citable source and matrix location or database representation were found, but the matrix was not independently transcribed and checked in Stage 1. `INACCESSIBLE` means a complete source-resolved matrix was not obtained in this sweep. Neither label is Stage-2 verification.

## Rejected in sweep

- **GoMexSI and raw GloBI interaction aggregations** — extensive Gulf feeding records, but not a bounded ≤25-node published community web. This repeats the earlier North America sweep’s reason for rejection.
- **Broad Mexican marine shelf/bay models** — La Paz Bay, Bahía Magdalena, Gulf of California, Campeche Bank and Yucatán shelf models surfaced, some with appendices, but marine already has six live webs. They were rejected early under the biome-saturation gate; Ascensión is retained above as the representative record because it is already in the open Mexico report and raw-size compliant.
- **Mexican freshwater lake/reservoir studies** — Chapala, Pátzcuaro, Catemaco and Valle de Bravo searches found assemblage, diet, isotope or trophic-status work rather than a complete community matrix. Zimapán Dam’s Dryad deposit covers eight fish species plus two subspecies with traits/isotopes, not a full edge list. Freshwater is also saturated with four live webs.
- **Central-Mexico carnivore feeding study and single-species diet papers** — useful diet overlap among pumas, jaguars and mesopredators, but only a predator-centred subweb without producers or a complete community matrix.
- **Chamela and other soil-food-web leads** — abundance, nematode/mite assemblage and soil-food-web-index studies did not yield a resolved consumer–resource matrix. Feeding-guild assignments cannot be expanded into edges without invention.
- **Mutualistic and host-only networks** — Campeche bird–fruit interactions and similar plant–animal datasets are outside the current whole-food-web product surface. Existing Mexican reef, desert-island and deep-sea candidates remain in their own files and were not rejected or duplicated here.
