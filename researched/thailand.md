# Region: Thailand — multi-biome country sweep
**Researched:** 2026-11-22 · **Status:** complete (Stage 1 — awaiting verification)

**Scope:** Thailand's estuaries and coastal waters, Andaman Sea coast, freshwater lakes/reservoirs, and terrestrial forests. This extends rather than re-runs the earlier Asia sweep. The already verified `gulf-of-thailand` candidate (Christensen 1998; 1963–64 pre-trawl model; Tier 2) remains owned by `verified/asia-research.md` and `verified/asia.md`; it was not re-scored or duplicated. The separate 1973 coastal Gulf model by Vibunpant et al. (2003) is recorded here as a different model period/source. Coral-reef-specific webs belong to the earlier coral-reef sweep.

**Repo context at sweep time:** 16 webs live; no implemented Thailand pin. Biome saturation: marine 6 and freshwater 3 (both saturated), grassland 3, estuary 2, wetland 1, desert 1. Estuarine and terrestrial candidates therefore have the clearest route through the geography gate. Strong marine and freshwater models are appendix-only under the current saturation rule.

**Verdict:** buildable — two Tier 1 estuary candidates (`mae-klong-estuary` and `bandon-bay`) with open weighted diet matrices and natural source groups. No Tier 2 candidate: the remaining records are appendix because of biome saturation, inaccessible matrices, or both. No resolved whole-forest community web was found; the Sakaerat spider–prey networks are useful but incomplete, request-only subwebs.

**Access terminology:** `ASSUMED` means a citable model and a plausible matrix location were found, but the matrix was not independently obtained/transcribed in Stage 1. `INACCESSIBLE` means the matrix could not be obtained in this sweep. Neither label is Stage-2 verification.

- **Scores:** data 3 · story 3 · geography 3 · cost 3 · recognition 1 = **13**

### Bandon Bay, Surat Thani — `bandon-bay`
- **Biome / Region:** Tropical estuarine bay and mangrove inlet · Gulf of Thailand · **Lat/Lng:** 9.20, 99.67 (published coordinates)
- **Source:** Sawusdee, A., Koolkalya, S., Thapanand, T. & Jutagate, T. (2023). Evolution of the Food Web in Bandon Bay, the Gulf of Thailand: Ten Years of the Blue Swimming Crab Stocking Programme. *Tropical Life Sciences Research* 34(2): 109–130. [DOI 10.21315/tlsr2023.34.2.6](https://doi.org/10.21315/tlsr2023.34.2.6); [open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10735257/).
- **Data access:** **ASSUMED (strong), not verified** — open article prints 2007 and 2016 diet matrices in Tables 4 and 5, with trophic levels and balanced inputs in Tables 2–3. Matrices were inspected at discovery level but not transcribed or independently checked.
- **Raw size:** 24 groups in 2007 (20 fish/shellfish + benthos, zooplankton, phytoplankton, detritus) and 26 in 2016 (22 + 4) / links not extracted · **Aggregation plan:** use 2007 → 24 nodes raw, or 2016 → 25 nodes by merging the taxonomically overlapping scads/Carangidae only after checking source definitions; never average the two periods into an invented web.
- **Data type:** weighted (Ecopath diet fractions) · **Provenance:** empirical (six trawl cruises in 2007 and ten in 2016 plus stomach-content reports and literature for basal groups)
- **Scores:** data 3 · story 3 · geography 3 · cost 2 · recognition 1 = **12**
- **Tier:** 1 · **Notes:** A rare intervention story: community/government crab banks began stocking blue swimming crab in 2007, and the paper compares the food web before and ten years after. Crab biomass rose while mantis shrimp was the only group negatively affected in the mixed-trophic-impact analysis; detritus–benthos pathways anchor the web. The two matrices also expose source-quality questions for Stage 2: Table 1 is incomplete/typo-prone, so group membership must be reconciled with Tables 2–5 rather than silently corrected. Stocking success and causation should not be overstated.
