# Region: Pakistan (multi-biome sweep)
**Researched:** 2026-11 · **Status:** complete (Stage 1 — NOT BUILDABLE; parked after follow-up sweep)

**Repo context at sweep time:** 10 webs live; Pakistan has one appendix ledger entry and no implemented pin. Biome saturation: freshwater 3 (saturated), marine 3 (saturated), grassland 2, estuary 1, wetland 1. Scope: Pakistan's Indus River, lakes and reservoirs; Himalayan/Karakoram/Hindu Kush terrestrial systems; Indus Delta and other coastal wetlands; and the Pakistani coast and margin of the northern Arabian Sea. The country was treated as one target, not split into continent-scale subregions.

**Outcome:** one genuine qualitative network was found for Rawal Lake, but it has only 11 compartments and therefore fails the 15-node minimum. Splitting its pooled compartments would require unsupported diet data. A follow-up sweep found additional process and diet studies but no new resolved Pakistan web. No Tier 1 or Tier 2 candidate is recommended.

## Appendix — recorded, not planned

### Rawal Lake, Margalla Hills National Park, Islamabad — `rawal-lake`
- **Biome / Region:** Artificial freshwater reservoir in subtropical Himalayan foothills · Pakistan · **Lat/Lng:** 33°42′N, 73°07′E (33.70, 73.12)
- **Source:** Amjad, M., Fath, B.D. & Rovenskaya, E. (2011). *Ecological Network Model and Analysis for Rawal Lake, Pakistan*. IIASA Interim Report IR-11-023, Laxenburg, Austria. [Open report](https://pure.iiasa.ac.at/id/eprint/9812/1/IR-11-023.pdf).
- **Data access:** ASSUMED — the open report and Table 2 adjacency matrix were located and inspected at discovery level; independent Stage 2 verification has not been performed. Table 2 is binary, not a measured diet matrix.
- **Raw size:** 11 functional compartments / 25 directed connections (report states 20% connectivity) · **Aggregation plan:** no valid plan. The network is already below the 15-node minimum; its pooled small-fish, large-fish, bird and other compartments cannot be split without additional sourced diet data.
- **Data type:** qualitative adjacency. Numerical flow coefficients were randomly generated between 0 and 1 for Monte Carlo stability experiments and must not be reused as empirical edge weights. · **Provenance:** composite (multiple literature/agency sources plus general biological knowledge).
- **Hard gates:** published agency report: pass · node/edge matrix obtainable: provisional pass pending Stage 2 · plausible 15–25-node version: **fail** · trophic structure: pass · biome not saturated: **fail** (freshwater already has 3 implemented webs).
- **Scores:** data 2 · story 2 · geography 2 · cost 1 · recognition 2 = **9**
- **Tier:** appendix · **Notes:** The only resolved Pakistan-wide food-web candidate found in this sweep. Potential stories are nutrient-driven eutrophication, fishing of large fish, and migratory birds in an Islamabad water-supply reservoir. However, the authors explicitly call it a crude first approximation requiring calibration and validation. Do not promote by inventing nodes or treating random simulation coefficients as observations.

## Rejected in sweep

- **Indus River at Kalabagh–Chashma–Attock (Mianwali), Punjab** — Mahboob, S. et al. (2019), “Impact of Water Pollution on Trophic Transfer of Fatty Acids in Fish, Microalgae, and Zoobenthos in the Food Web of a Freshwater Ecosystem,” *Biomolecules* 9(6):231, [DOI 10.3390/biom9060231](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom9060231). Open full text samples periphyton, zoobenthos and three carp species at three pollution levels; it reports water, metal and fatty-acid measurements, not a resolved node/edge matrix. Five sampled compartments fail minimum richness; freshwater is also saturated.
- **Pakistan Margin deep-sea megafauna, northern Arabian Sea** — Jeffreys, R.M., Wolff, G.A. & Murty, S.J. (2009), “The trophic ecology of key megafaunal species at the Pakistan Margin: Evidence from stable isotopes and lipid biomarkers,” *Deep-Sea Research I* 56:1816–1833, [DOI 10.1016/j.dsr.2009.05.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dsr.2009.05.001). Valuable trophic ecology, but stable-isotope/lipid evidence for selected benthic megafauna is not a published whole-web adjacency or diet matrix. No aggregation-ready web found.
- **Miani Hor / Sonmiani Bay, Balochistan** — searches found zooplankton, intertidal-fauna, finfish-feeding and lagoon-morphodynamics studies, but no resolved food web or Ecopath model. The biodiversity and migratory-dolphin story is strong, but the required matrix is absent; do not reconstruct one from inventories.
- **Indus Delta mangrove tidal creeks, Sindh** — Bano, N., Nisa, M.-U., Khan, N., Saleem, M., Harrison, P.J., Ahmed, S.I. & Azam, F. (1997), “Significance of bacteria in the flux of organic matter in the tidal creeks of the mangrove ecosystem of the Indus River delta, Pakistan,” *Marine Ecology Progress Series* 157:1–12, [DOI 10.3354/meps157001](https://doi.org/10.3354/meps157001). The open paper measures bacterial and primary production and includes a conceptual material/energy-flow diagram, not a resolved whole-food-web adjacency or diet matrix. It cannot support a 15–25-node build without inventing nodes and links.
- **Keenjhar (Kalri) and Manchar lakes, Sindh** — limnological, biodiversity and pollution studies only; no resolved food-web matrix found. Freshwater saturation is an additional gate.
- **Rawal Dam fish-population study** — Afzal, M., Rab, A., Akhtar, N. & Yaqoob, M. (1995), “Status and management of fish populations of Rawal Dam reservoir, Pakistan,” *Fisheries Management and Ecology* 2(1):37–41, [DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2400.1995.tb00096.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2400.1995.tb00096.x). This is a fish-population source used by the 2011 network report, not a separately confirmed food web. Wiley full text was inaccessible; no matrix claimed.
- **Northeastern Arabian Sea Ecopath model (2024)** — open 23-group report has an explicit Maharashtra and Gujarat, India boundary. Out of geographic scope; not evidence of a Pakistan model.
- **Kaptai Lake Ecopath model (2026)** — open 15-group weighted diet matrix, but Kaptai Lake is in Bangladesh. Out of scope; could be considered in a separate Bangladesh/South Asia sweep, subject to freshwater saturation.
- **Lake Annecy Ecopath model (Janjua & Gerdeaux 2009)** — false positive from Pakistan Higher Education Commission funding; study site is France.
- **Oman Sea Ecopath models** — false positives from the journal name *Pakistan Journal of Agricultural Research* and Pakistani coauthors. In particular, Doustdar, M., Hashemi, S.A.R., Muhammad, A.A. & Forouzad, M. (2024), “Investigation of ecological relationships among some economical species in the Oman Sea using Ecopath model,” *Pakistan Journal of Agricultural Research* 37(4):373–383, [DOI 10.17582/journal.pjar/2024/37.4.373.383](https://doi.org/10.17582/journal.pjar/2024/37.4.373.383), explicitly sampled Bazm, Konarak, Chabahar, Ramin and Beris in Sistan and Baluchestan Province, Iran. It is out of geographic scope, not evidence for a Pakistan model.
- **GloBI fungus–host records and individual fish diet records** — interaction records exist, but they are not a coherent, published Pakistan ecosystem food web and do not meet the project's whole-web provenance requirement.

## Recommendation

Do not schedule a Pakistan build from this sweep. The file is parked under `researched/notbuildable/`; do not repeat the sweep unless new evidence explicitly warrants it. If the project later relaxes the 15-node minimum or commissions a properly sourced expansion of the Rawal Lake model, Stage 2 should first verify Table 2 and every compartment/link against IR-11-023. Otherwise, a future Bangladesh-focused sweep has a stronger immediately available lead in Kaptai Lake, while Pakistan remains a genuine resolved-web data gap rather than a curation opportunity.
