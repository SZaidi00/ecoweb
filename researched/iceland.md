### Vatnajökull nunatak arthropod webs, SE Iceland — `vatnajokull-nunataks`
- **Biome / Region:** Sub-Arctic/alpine terrestrial glacier foreland and nunataks · Vatnajökull, SE Iceland · **Lat/Lng (approx):** 64.0, −17.2
- **Source:** Ingimarsdóttir, M., Ripa, J., Magnúsdóttir, Ó.B. & Hedlund, K. (2013). Food web assembly in isolated habitats: A study from recently emerged nunataks, Iceland. *Basic and Applied Ecology* 14(2): 174–183. [DOI 10.1016/j.baae.2012.12.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.baae.2012.12.002); [article record and abstract](https://portal.research.lu.se/sv/publications/food-web-assembly-in-isolated-habitats-a-study-from-recently-emer). Supporting resource-use study: Ingimarsdóttir, M., Michelsen, A., Ripa, J. & Hedlund, K. (2014). Food sources of early colonising arthropods: The importance of allochthonous input. *Pedobiologia* 57(1): 21–26. [DOI 10.1016/j.pedobi.2013.09.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pedobi.2013.09.004).
- **Data access:** ASSUMED (moderate) — the article reports 17,874 arthropods, 106 species/taxa and 27 trophic groups, with Appendix A Table 1 and food-web diagrams in Figure 2. No machine-readable link matrix was located, and the full appendix/figures have not yet been extracted. Stage 2 must establish whether every drawn edge is supported by the source rather than inferred from guild membership.
- **Raw size:** 106 taxa / 27 trophic groups across six nunataks and successional ages · **Aggregation plan:** select one sufficiently rich site/age and reduce 27 source groups → ~18–22 nodes; do not pool nunataks or ages merely to reach the node minimum
- **Data type:** qualitative trophic-group links; biomass and abundance are available separately · **Provenance:** composite (field community samples plus trophic classifications); direct feeding observations are not claimed
- **Scores:** data 1 · story 3 · geography 3 · cost 2 · recognition 2 = **11**
- **Tier:** 2 (high-curation-risk) · **Notes:** The only candidate passing the current biome-saturation gate. The story is ecosystem assembly on newly exposed land: wind-blown arthropods subsidise detritivores and predators before vegetation establishes, so removing allochthonous dipterans could expose a dependence invisible in species lists. The major risk is provenance: trophic-level assignments and guild diagrams are not equivalent to measured pairwise diets. Stage 2 must inspect Figure 2 and Appendix A at full resolution, choose a single coherent web, and keep transient allochthonous taxa distinct from established consumers.

- **Biome / Region:** Sub-Arctic/boreal North Atlantic shelf and EEZ · Iceland · **Lat/Lng (approx):** 65.0, −18.0
- **Source:** Sonjudóttir, A., Sturludóttir, E. & Elvarsson, B.Þ. (2024). *Modelling the food web in Icelandic waters*. Marine and Freshwater Research Institute, HV 2024-08, ISSN 2298-9137. [Open report](https://www.hafogvatn.is/static/research/files/modelling-the-food-web-in-icelandic-waters.pdf). Earlier model: Ribeiro, J.P.C., Elvarsson, B.Þ., Sturludóttir, E. & Stefánsson, G. (2018). An overview of the marine food web in Icelandic waters using Ecopath with Ecosim. [arXiv:1810.00613](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00613).
- **Data access:** ASSUMED (strong) — the open 2024 report prints the balanced weighted diet matrix as Tables 30–32 (pp. 78–80) and documents the functional groups and data sources. Matrix not yet transcribed or independently checked. The 2018 model has 35 functional groups and open appendices, but the newer report is the preferred source.
- **Raw size:** 36 model compartments (35 living groups plus detritus) in the 2024 matrix · **Aggregation plan:** → ~20–25 nodes (retain cod life stages where useful, capelin, herring, mackerel, whales/seals, seabirds, shrimp, benthos, zooplankton and producers; merge only along source functional-group boundaries)
- **Data type:** weighted diet proportions and Ecopath flows · **Provenance:** empirical (documented model using extensive MFRI stomach, survey and literature inputs)
- **Scores:** data 3 · story 3 · geography 3 · cost 2 · recognition 3 = **14**
- **Tier:** appendix (biome-saturation gate) · **Notes:** Numerically the strongest candidate in the sweep. Capelin links zooplankton to cod, seabirds and marine mammals; warming-driven mackerel arrival and the cod–shrimp interaction provide additional cascade stories. Marine already has three live webs. If that gate changes, this is the first Icelandic candidate to verify. The older EcoBase model 66 (`Iceland 1997`, 24 groups) and the 1950 reconstruction are alternative historical baselines, not separate candidate ecosystems.

- **Source:** O'Gorman, E.J. et al. (2019). A simple model predicts how warming simplifies wild food webs. *Nature Climate Change* 9: 611–616. [DOI 10.1038/s41558-019-0513-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-019-0513-x). Experimental extension: O'Gorman, E.J., Zhao, L., Kordas, R.L., Dudgeon, S. & Woodward, G. (2023). Warming indirectly simplifies food webs through effects on apex predators. *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 7: 1983–1992. [DOI 10.1038/s41559-023-02216-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41559-023-02216-4); [open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10697836/); [data/code DOI 10.5526/ERDR-00000186](https://researchdata.essex.ac.uk/186/).
- **Data access:** ASSUMED (strong) — the Essex record provides `Hengill Fish Exclusion Experiment.zip` and documents food-web construction from 49,324 gut-content observations. The 2012 review also names Appendix D as the source of food-web links. The zip and earlier appendices have not yet been extracted, so an auditable per-stream edge list remains a Stage-2 gate.
- **Raw size:** 14 stream webs in the thermal-gradient lineage; 49,324 gut-content observations; per-web node/link counts not yet extracted · **Aggregation plan:** select a naturally 15–25-node stream web if available; otherwise aggregate diatoms and invertebrates only along demonstrated source groups, without pooling streams merely to reach richness
- **Data type:** mixed — observed qualitative links with diet/biomass information; 2023 webs use 58% stream-specific observations, 35% observations from other Hengill streams and 7% literature links · **Provenance:** composite (empirical gut contents plus cross-stream and literature supplementation)
- **Scores:** data 3 · story 3 · geography 3 · cost 2 · recognition 1 = **12**
- **Tier:** appendix (biome-saturation gate) · **Notes:** Brown trout sit above a geothermal natural experiment in which warming and predator presence jointly alter connectance, algal production and decomposition. This is an excellent cascade dataset, but freshwater already has three live webs. Do not mix the 14-stream baseline, the six-stream fish-exclusion experiment and the 2026 core-reorganisation analysis into a synthetic web.
